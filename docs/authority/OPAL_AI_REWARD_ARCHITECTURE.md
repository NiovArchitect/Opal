## Core distinction

This is **larger than gamification**.

Opal’s reward system is an **AI-mediated reward architecture**: the product continuously converts context, conversation, location, preferences, relationships, timing, memory, and intent into increasingly relevant possibilities, then makes the path from possibility to real-world experience extremely short.

Opal optimizes for **meaningful reward and successful outcomes**, not compulsive engagement.

The highest-value “dopamine hit” is **not** another notification. It is:

- “That is exactly what I wanted.”
- “I didn’t have to explain myself.”
- “We actually made this happen.”
- “This night turned out incredible.”
- “Opal knew what would work for us.”
- “I’m alone in a new place, but somehow I know what to do next.”

Reward moments come from **recognition, anticipation, convergence, serendipity, reciprocity, commitment, reality, and memory** — because life became easier or more interesting.

That is harder to copy than badges.

## Reward layers (compounding)

### A. Visual reward

Brand V4 + approved Figma composition create visual anticipation **before** the user acts. Visual dominance is product function, not decoration.

Do not flatten Opal into generic SaaS UI, utilitarian cards, excessive text, or chatbot conventions when Figma specifies otherwise.

### B. Intelligence reward

The system demonstrates understanding: recalls preference, who is involved, location, timeframe, existing plan, prior interest, avoids re-asking answered questions, notices meaningful overlap, uses correct Graph/Journey/message context.

### C. Anticipation reward

Something compelling could happen (tonight’s possibilities, friends who would join, unusually good fit, live music nearby, tangible future trip).

### D. Alignment reward

Multiple constraints converge into “Of course. That makes sense.” — not a dump of four facts.

### E. Commitment reward

Possibility becomes real. Preserve: Interested ≠ Going · Follow ≠ Connection · Group ≠ Journey. Moving to “I’m going” must feel meaningful because **social reality changed**.

### F. Social reciprocity reward

Someone joins, responds, aligns, follows through; group reaches sufficient agreement; alternate suggestion that actually fits.

### G. Discovery reward

Reveals something the user did not know but is genuinely relevant. **Not randomness.** Serendipity constrained by fit.

### H. Completion reward

Plan happened; Journey concludes; experience enters memory and strengthens future recommendations.

### I. Memory reward

Next interaction is easier because Opal learned. Memory reduces future friction — it is not archival storage alone.

### J. Surprise reward

Occasionally an unusually good option slightly outside the obvious, still bounded by relevance. Do not confuse novelty with quality.

## Solo reward is not a fallback

Solo intelligence must produce first-class reward moments (“I know what to do next here”) without requiring a social graph. Group participation multiplies value; it does not unlock the first useful experience.

---

## Outcome orientation

Reward architecture must stay tied to **successful real-world outcomes** and consented learning (including negative outcomes). A system that only remembers likes and never what repeatedly failed will feel dumb.

---

## Visual dominance vs friction

Require both:

- A beautiful interface that obstructs alignment is wrong.  
- A frictionless interface that loses Opal’s emotional identity is also wrong.

Motion may support anticipation and continuity; avoid gratuitous motion that delays action.
