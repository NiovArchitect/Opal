# Audience research — initial wedge hypothesis

## Women-wedge evidence (directional)
- Pew Research Center (2025): women stand out in use of Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok among U.S. adults; e.g. Instagram 55% women vs 44% men (use rates). Daily platform habits also show strong Facebook/YouTube daily use overall.
- Research tradition on mental load: women often disproportionately carry planning/coordination labor — maps to Opal’s “reduce invisible social work” value proposition.
- Creator economy: large female share in lifestyle/influence categories (marketplace reports such as Collabstr-style analyses) → experience-catalyst creators as a channel.

## Limitations
- Platform-use ≠ Opal product-market fit.
- Must not design a “women-only” product or stereotype.
- Men and mixed groups remain first-class.

## Alternative wedges
College/young-professional friend groups; couples + triads; fitness/church/hobby clusters; single-city density pilot.

## Recommended initial cohort
Socially active **18–34 friend groups** (mixed gender), with **women-dense social graphs as a distribution hypothesis**, not a product identity.

## Research plan
10s comprehension tests; invitation rate; three-person activation; first experience completion (synthetic ok); privacy trust; “would show a friend.”
