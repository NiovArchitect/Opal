Women as initial wedge — hypothesis, not stereotype

Pew Research (2025) finds women stand out on several platforms (e.g. higher Instagram use than men; elevated presence on Facebook and TikTok usage patterns). That supports testing socially active women as an early cohort for invitation and coordination products — not designing Opal “for women only.”

EvidenceImplicationLimit
Higher female use of major social platformsDistribution channels & invitation behavior may seed fasterNot product-fit proof
Mental-load / coordination labor (research tradition)Opal’s “reduce invisible planning work” may resonateMust not imply women alone plan
Creator marketplace often woman-heavy in lifestyle categoriesExperience catalysts in food, wellness, local lifeDo not gate creators by gender

Alternative wedges to test in parallel

  • College / young-professional friend groups (food, events, trips)
  • Couples + close friend triads (high completion rate potential)
  • Fitness / church / hobby clusters with recurring meetups
  • City-density pilot (one metro) for local inventory later
